Evaluating a 2-for-1 trade is one of the most common challenges in fantasy baseball. When managers evaluate trades, they often make the mistake of looking purely at the combined projections.

For example, if you trade one elite player for two good players, it might seem like a win on paper if the two players combine for slightly more points. However, this ignores a fundamental constraint of fantasy baseball: **roster size**.

## The Replacement Level Concept

Whenever you execute a 2-for-1 trade, the team receiving two players must drop a player to make room on their active roster. Therefore, the true equation isn't `Player A vs Player B + Player C`.

The actual trade is:
**Player A + Replacement Player vs Player B + Player C**

### League Depth Matters

The value of that "Replacement Player" changes drastically depending on your league size:

1. **8-Team Leagues**: The waiver wire is rich with talent. A replacement player might be a reliable contributor. In this scenario, the elite side of a 2-for-1 (Player A) is heavily favored.
2. **16-Team Leagues**: The waiver wire is barren. A replacement player might provide almost zero value. Here, acquiring depth (Players B and C) is much more viable.

## Analyzing Categories vs Points

Different formats demand different valuation methods.

| Format | Key Strategy | Value Focus |
|--------|--------------|-------------|
| Roto (5x5) | Category Balance | Scarcity (Saves, Steals) |
| Points | Volume | Total Production |

When building trades, ensure you understand how the scoring format impacts player values. An elite base-stealer might be a first-round pick in Roto, but only a mid-round asset in Points leagues.
